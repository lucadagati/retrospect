Dear Dr. Bruno, Dr. Chounos, and Dr. Ilager,

Please find enclosed our manuscript entitled "Kubernetes-Native Cloud--Fog--Edge Orchestration for WebAssembly-Enabled Embedded IoT Devices" for consideration as an original research article in the Computer Communications special issue "Sustainable Digital Research Infrastructures for the Edge-to-Cloud Continuum".

The manuscript addresses the special issue theme from the perspective of virtualization and orchestration for edge-to-cloud research infrastructures. It presents a Kubernetes-native framework that allows constrained embedded IoT endpoints to participate in declarative orchestration without becoming Kubernetes nodes. The framework combines Custom Resource Definitions, gateway-mediated TLS/CBOR communication, Zephyr-based device support, and WebAssembly Micro Runtime execution to provide a reproducible, observable, and instrumentation-ready control path across cloud, fog, and edge layers.

We believe the work is relevant to the special issue because sustainable digital research infrastructures require repeatable experiment control, explicit evidence traceability, and lightweight edge participation before energy-aware placement, power instrumentation, or carbon-aware policies can be evaluated reliably. The current manuscript does not claim measured energy or carbon reductions; instead, it contributes the orchestration and evidence substrate needed to support green experimentation and future sustainability-aware management of constrained edge resources.

The main contributions are:

1. A gateway-centric Cloud--Fog--Edge architecture integrating Kubernetes CRDs with constrained-device runtime control.
2. A secure southbound protocol workflow based on TLS and CBOR for enrollment, identity matching, liveness supervision, and deployment evidence.
3. A full implementation with Rust services, Zephyr/WAMR edge runtime integration, and reproducible K3s deployment.
4. An end-to-end validation campaign showing consistent enrollment, heartbeat supervision, and WASM deployment across 100 independent trials.
5. A positioning of the framework as a reproducible and measurement-ready substrate for sustainable edge-to-cloud research infrastructures.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the manuscript and agree with its submission to Computer Communications.
